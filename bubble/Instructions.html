You will play a short trading game many times in groups of five. All the other traders in your games are real participants in this room. In each game the five traders are placed in a random line, and you will not know your place when you decide.

An asset is offered to the first trader for ${{ C.START_PRICE }}, and then to each trader in turn:

  • If a trader buys, the asset is offered to the next trader at twice the price.
  • If a trader does not buy, the asset is offered to the next trader at the same price.

Every trader is offered the asset once. The asset itself pays nothing. Its only value is the chance to resell it to a trader later in line.

Your position in line

The first trader is always offered the asset at ${{ C.START_PRICE }}. Because a refused asset is passed on at the same price, the price you are offered usually does not tell you your position, so you usually cannot be sure anyone will buy after you.

Deciding and getting paid

In each game you say whether you would buy at every price you could be offered. Only your decision at the price you are actually offered counts.

You will play one unpaid practice game, then two parts of {{ C.GAMES_PER_PART }} games each. You are placed in a new random group and a new place in line in every game. Right after each game you will see what happened and how much you earned in that game.

One of the {{ C.GAMES_PER_PART }} × 2 paid games was chosen at random for this session. Everyone in the room is paid for that same game, and it is revealed at the end. Since any game could be the one that counts, treat each one as if it determines your payment.
